Culture of Tierralta

Traditions

The traditions of the municipality of Tierralta, Córdoba, are diverse. On the one hand, there are those of our ancestors, some of whom remain to this day, such as those of the Embera Kaito indigenous community, with their ancestral customs and traditional dances. On the one hand, there are dances and music like the fandango and the cumbia, and on the other hand, there are festivities such as Holy Week.

1. Fandango

This music, performed by a folk band, is used for dancing in parades. Women wear polleras (traditional skirts), and men wear vueltiao hats. Lit candles are also used in this dance, which children participate in, keeping the ancestral heritage alive.

2. Cumbia

This vibrant folkloric expression from the savanna region of the Caribbean is a circular partner dance accompanied by brass bands. The women dance wearing a pollera, or wide skirt, and holding candles as a courtship gesture, while the men follow elegantly. In this dance, they circle the musical group counterclockwise.

3. Vallenato

This music is characterized by the use of the accordion, the caja (a type of drum), and the guacharaca (a percussion instrument). It is danced in pairs with a rhythmic, courtship-like movement to the beat of traditional styles such as paseo, merengue, and puya.

Heritage

The cultural and natural heritage of Tierralta is reflected in its abundant natural environment, including its well-preserved flora and fauna. It is also evident in the traditions still preserved, such as dance, music, recipes, handicrafts, and the lithic tools and ceramic fragments of the Zenú people that have been preserved.

1. Zenú Archaeological Museum

This Zenú archaeological museum in Tierralta boasts a large collection of Zenú ceramics, as well as lithic tools. You can also find bones, photographs, and other artifacts related to the Zenú people.

2. Handicrafts

The handicrafts of Tierralta are primarily known for the production of the Ebera Neka Handicrafts Association, backpacks, hats, bags, and other items, using ancestral techniques combined with innovation.

3. Embera Katio Dance

These ancestral dances remain to this day, and some of them help us remember our connection with nature and how important and fundamental it is to us.
