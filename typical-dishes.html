Typical Dishes

The region’s dishes are known for being home-style, hearty, and full of flavor. They generally include side dishes such as rice, plantains, cassava, potatoes, or beans, along with proteins like pork, chicken, beef, or fish.
They are often complemented with fresh salads, avocado, and traditional beverages, offering balanced meals that are representative of Colombia Caribbean cuisine.

Mote de Queso (Cheese Soup)

Mote de queso is one of Tierralta’s most representative dishes. It’s a thick soup made with yam and cubed cheese, seasoned with ingredients like onion, garlic, and lime.
Depending on the preparation, it may include cilantro, tomato, scallions, or whey, which gives it an even more distinctive flavor. This dish is often served with chicharron (fried pork rinds), avocado, patacones (fried plantains), or slices of plantain.

Bocachico (Fish)

Bocachico is a typical fish of the region and one of the most consumed in Tierralta. It can be prepared fried, grilled, or in soup, always retaining its characteristic flavor.
It’s usually served with rice, plantain, yucca, yam, salad, and avocado, making it delicious and representative of the local cuisine.

Machucado (mashed plantains)

Machucado (mashed plantains), also known as “cabeza de gato” (cat’s head), is a traditional dish primarily eaten for breakfast. They are made from cooked plantains that are then mashed until smooth.
They can be served as a paste or in small portions and are complemented with ingredients such as oil, onion, and garlic. It is also common to serve them with eggs, cheese, butter, pork rinds, ribs, or sausages, making them a nutritious and energizing option.
